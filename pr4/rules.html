Or ask for guidance.

Organize

Each round, first meld one complete legal formation from your own hand. After that, you may extend or rearrange Heaven, including on that same turn. Finish every formation legally. Organizing powers do not waive the opening requirement.

Or draw

Your Favor determines how many random bag souls you may inspect. Keep one and return and mix the rest. Drawing ends your turn. You may draw even if you could play.

Deliver souls from your hand to earn Favor: add three souls, gain three Favor. Rearranging alone earns none.

More Favor, more choice

MIRACLES, REGIONS & THE FINAL JUDGMENT

The table tells the story.

Sanctuary

Everything inside is protected. Additions become protected; removed souls become public. You may leave a legal three-tile Choir safe while using its fourth tile elsewhere.

Purgatory

Raphael may place one excess table soul here during hand play. Purgatory does not prevent going out. On later turns, rescue into a Procession or draw.

Scales commitments

Keep chosen tiles separate on your rack. Delivery earns an immediate, permanent printed-rank credit. Once delivered, a committed tile is ordinary. No tile history is recorded.

THE COMPLETE AUTHORITATIVE REFERENCE

All of Heaven’s rules.

1. Objective and components

For 3–5 players, over three rounds. Finish with the highest cumulative score. In each round, deliver your souls into Heaven and try to be the first player with an empty hand. Purgatory does not prevent going out.

Use:

  • A standard 106-tile Rummikub set: ranks 1–13 in four colors, two copies of each numbered tile, plus two jokers.
  • Twelve distinct Angel cards, ten ordinary Heavenly Decree cards, and five Final Judgment cards, as described below.
  • An opaque draw bag, private hands or racks, visible Divine Favor tracks, a score sheet, and indicators for used powers. Identify commitments by a separate area on each owner’s rack.
  • Clearly marked, visible Sanctuary and Purgatory areas where required.

Every tile, including a joker, is a soul. Heaven includes the shared table and all Sanctuaries. Hands and Purgatory are outside Heaven. A Sanctuary is private in control, not hidden from view. A Purgatory holds at most one soul.

2. Game and round setup

  1. Randomly determine a first player for round one. Play clockwise. The first player moves one seat clockwise for each later round.
  2. Shuffle the Angel cards and deal two to each player privately. Each player chooses one. Reveal the chosen Angels together once everyone has decided, and set all unchosen and undealt Angels aside. Keep your chosen Angel throughout all three rounds.
  3. Shuffle the ten ordinary Decrees. Use the top card in round one and the next card in round two, without replacement. Shuffle the five Final Judgments and use one in round three. Only the current round's card applies.
  4. At the start of each round, return all 106 souls to the bag and mix them. Clear Heaven and all special zones. Reset powers to unused and Favor to zero. Deal each player 14 random souls privately. Everyone must make a new opening meld each round; place your Angel above your rack to show you have not opened.
  5. Reveal the current round's decree before anyone takes a turn. In round three, resolve any setup instructions after players have seen their hands and before the first turn. Previous ordinary decrees do not carry into round three.

The starting player, seats, cumulative scores, and Angel assignments persist as specified above; tile locations, commitments, and Sanctuary protection do not.

3. Legal formations

Unless the active decree changes them, Heaven permits two formations:

Formation Requirements
Choir Exactly 3 or 4 souls of one rank, all different colors.
Procession At least 3 souls of one color in consecutive ascending ranks.

For example, red 7 / blue 7 / black 7 is a Choir; blue 4–5–6–7 is a Procession. Ordinary Processions do not wrap from 13 to 1. Each tile belongs to exactly one formation; formations do not share tiles.

A decree replaces only the requirements it explicitly changes. All other requirements remain. God's Poker Night explicitly adds formations and is the exception to replacement. The complete decree definitions in section 8 govern over examples or card shorthand.

Rearranging Heaven

There is no 30-point opening requirement. Before extending or rearranging Heaven, you must make an opening meld: one complete legal formation entirely from your own hand. Follow the active decree; jokers are allowed normally. No souls from Heaven or an opponent may help complete your opening.

Open once per round. After placing that formation, you may immediately play single souls, rearrange unprotected Heaven, and use organizing powers in the same Organize action. On later turns, no new complete hand formation is required. Organizing powers cannot bypass opening; other powers retain their normal timing.

Show an unopened Angel above your rack. Once opened, move it beside your Favor track; turning it sideways still shows a used power. Reset its opening position each round. No extra marker is needed.

At the end of every completed action, every soul in Heaven must belong to a legal formation. You may not take souls from Heaven into a hand or Purgatory unless a power explicitly allows it. Sanctuary ownership limits who may move its tiles.

If an attempted Organize action cannot be completed legally, restore all tiles and zones to their positions before the attempt, cancel powers attempted as part of that action, and draw instead. There is no additional penalty or turn timer. An earlier, separately completed power remains resolved and spent. An opening meld made only in the canceled action does not count; restore its opening status.

Jokers

A joker represents one numbered soul of a specified rank and color that makes its formation legal. State its identity when placing it. Both jokers may appear in a formation if every represented position satisfies the active rules.

During an Organize action, you may replace a joker with a numbered soul from your hand that is legal in its place. If several identities are possible, any legal replacement is sufficient. The exchange itself earns zero Favor. You must reuse the recovered joker in a legal formation in Heaven during that same action; you may change its represented identity. You may not retain it in your hand. Simply rearranging a joker already in Heaven also earns no Favor.

Powers that explicitly retrieve a soul may retrieve a joker into a hand or Purgatory without requiring immediate replay. Opponents cannot recover a joker from your Sanctuary. A joker in a hand or Purgatory is worth 30 penalty points. Jokers have no rank while outside Heaven and cannot be called or banished by rank.

4. Taking a turn and ending a round

Choose one ordinary action:

  • Organize: Contribute at least one soul from your hand and finish Heaven legally. A successful Forgiveness, Penance, Sanctuary, Communion, or Transfiguration activation may instead supply this action, subject to its own conditions, even if you contribute no hand soul where that power allows it. On your opening turn, first meld a complete formation from your own hand as described above; organizing powers never waive that requirement.
  • Draw: Inspect the number of bag souls allowed by Divine Favor, keep one, and return the others. You may draw even if you could play. Drawing ends your turn; the drawn soul cannot be played on that turn.

Rearrangement alone, without a hand contribution or successful organizing power, does not complete an Organize action. If you do neither, you must draw.

Your Angel power is usable once per round. Turn its card sideways after a successful activation. Timing is specified in section 7. No power interrupts another player's action. Draw effects from powers are not ordinary Draw actions unless stated. Transfers between hands earn no Favor.

At the completion of every action or power, check whether any player has an empty hand. That player goes out and the round ends immediately, before another action or extra turn. Purgatory may remain occupied. Resolve an Organize action, including Penance and all rearrangement, as a whole before this check; do not interrupt it when the last hand tile is placed. Heaven must be legal for anyone to go out.

If a completed power empties several eligible players' hands simultaneously, its active player wins if eligible; otherwise the first eligible player clockwise after the active player wins. A player can therefore go out on another player's turn, including through Calling. Settle Favor from the completed action before scoring, but do not continue the turn.

5. Divine Favor

Favor resets to zero each round, except for a Final Judgment head start. Earn Favor for net deliveries during your action; rearranging or exchanging existing souls does not itself earn Favor. No individual tile histories are kept.

Forgiveness: Taking a soul into your hand with Forgiveness costs no Favor and does not offset Favor earned by other deliveries in the same action. Playing that soul again, in the same action or on a later turn, earns Favor normally. This is an intended benefit of Zadkiel's power. Do not mark or track the soul.

Examples:

  • Add three hand souls: +3.
  • Add four and retrieve one with Forgiveness: +4.
  • Use Forgiveness without adding any souls: 0.
  • Replay the Forgiveness soul in the same action or on a later turn: +1.
  • Exchange two hand souls for two table souls with Transfiguration: 0.
  • Rearrange Heaven or move a formation to or from a Sanctuary: 0.
  • Replace a joker and replay it elsewhere in Heaven: +1 overall, because the replacement is one additional tile in Heaven; the exchange step alone is 0.

Favor never decreases during a round. Transfiguration's two retrieved souls offset two contributions within its action; additional contributions earn Favor normally. Souls retrieved into a hand earn Favor normally when played on later turns, without tracking their earlier delivery. Communion credits its active player for both their contribution and the opponent's delivered soul.

Purgatory exception: Penance does not remove the soul's existing contribution from Favor, and rescuing it earns no new Favor. It remains attributed as before while in Purgatory, without recording its identity or its original contributor. It remains Raphael's responsibility for scoring if another player goes out, but does not prevent Raphael from going out.

Drawing with Favor

Immediately before an ordinary draw, compare all players' current Favor totals. Your inspection allowance is N minus the number of other players with Favor greater than or equal to yours, where N is the player count. Tied players share the lowest inspection allowance occupied by their tied group. Thus all players tied at zero inspect one soul each. In a four-player game with Favor 5, 5, 2, 0, the allowances are respectively 3, 3, 2, 1.

Randomly take up to that allowance from the bag, inspect privately, choose exactly one to keep, and return and mix the rest. If fewer remain, inspect all remaining souls. Uriel replaces this allowance with eight for one draw. Other blind power draws receive no Favor selection.

6. Empty bag and stalemate

If the bag is empty, a Draw action becomes a pass. Record consecutive turns in which no tile changes zone, no formation changes, and no power resolves. Any such change resets that count. When the bag is empty and the count reaches the number of players, the round is a stalemate and ends. Extra turns count as turns for this purpose. Do not end the round merely because the last bag soul was drawn.

In a stalemate, calculate each player's undelivered penalty as in section 10. The lowest penalty wins the round; ties go to the first tied player clockwise from and including that round's starting player. For each opponent, subtract the winner's penalty from that opponent's penalty. Each opponent loses that difference; the winner scores the sum of those differences. A shared minimum therefore produces zero loss for the other tied players. Apply any Final Judgment credits afterward.

For Final Judgment wording, the stalemate winner counts as the round winner and as the player who went out. Shared Sins uses each selected player's actual loss under this stalemate calculation, with zero for the winner.

7. The twelve Angels

Zadkiel — Forgiveness

During your Organize action, take one unprotected soul from Heaven into your hand. You may contribute other souls and rearrange Heaven during that action. You may also replay the retrieved soul in the same action; that delivery earns Favor normally, with no retrieval cost. The final formations must be legal. Taking the soul is permitted even if you contribute no souls. Retrieval costs no Favor, including when you also deliver other souls during the action. The retrieved soul earns Favor normally if played on the same or a later turn.

Raphael — Penance

During your Organize action, remove one unprotected soul from Heaven into your Purgatory. You may play hand souls and rearrange Heaven as part of this same action, including before and after moving the soul into Purgatory. Finish Heaven legally. Activating Penance consumes the power for the round.

For example, play your remaining hand into Heaven, move one excess table soul into Purgatory to make the remaining formations legal, and finish the action. Your hand is empty, so you go out even though Purgatory is occupied.

If the round continues, then on later turns while Purgatory is occupied your ordinary action is either to rescue that soul into a legal Procession or to draw. You may rearrange unprotected Heaven to rescue it. Once rescued, the restriction ends immediately: you may also play hand souls in that same Organize action. A failed rescue is restored and followed by a draw. The soul stays in Purgatory until rescued; it may not move to your hand or another player. An unrecovered soul counts in your penalty if another player goes out, but never prevents your own empty-hand finish. Its Favor treatment is in section 5.

Selaphiel — Sanctuary of Choirs

During your Organize action, move one complete legal Choir from shared Heaven into your visible Sanctuary. This may be a Choir completed during that action. Only you may move, add to, split, or rearrange protected tiles; any formations remaining in your Sanctuary must be legal Choirs. You may rearrange them together with unprotected Heaven on your turns. Moving them into your hand still requires a power that explicitly permits retrieval.

Your Sanctuary is a special physical region on the table. All tiles currently inside it are protected, including tiles you add from your hand or shared Heaven. Only you may rearrange or extend it, and all formations remaining inside must be legal Choirs at the end of the action.

You may move tiles out into shared Heaven during your Organize action. They are then available to everyone; protection is determined solely by current location, with no permanent released status or original-tile records. For example, from a four-tile Choir you may leave three safely inside Sanctuary and use the fourth in shared Heaven. If the Sanctuary is empty when your Organize action finishes, its once-per-round protection ends: remove its region marker, and do not establish another Sanctuary that round. While it remains occupied, you may add tiles and rearrange its contents freely. You may temporarily empty it while rearranging within one action, provided it contains legal formations when that action ends. Once closed, you cannot establish another Sanctuary that round.

Barachiel — Sanctuary of Processions

Use Selaphiel's rules, replacing Choir with Procession throughout.

Chamuel — Communion

During your Organize action, choose an opponent. Privately look at their entire hand, including any Scales commitments, and choose one soul. Keep any Scales commitments in their separate group while inspecting the hand. Reveal your chosen soul. You may deliver it directly into Heaven provided you also deliver at least one soul from your own hand in that action. You cannot keep the chosen soul in your hand. If you decline or cannot use it, return it to its owner and finish a normal Organize action or draw instead. The power is spent once you look at the opponent's hand, even if no soul is played. If the Organize attempt is rolled back, return the chosen soul but keep the power spent.

Jophiel — Transfiguration

During your Organize action, take exactly two unprotected souls from Heaven into your hand and deliver at least two souls that were in your hand before the action. The two retrieved souls must remain in your hand until this action ends; they cannot satisfy the delivery requirement. Other rearrangement and additional contributions are allowed. Finish Heaven legally and apply net Favor.

Uriel — Foretelling

When taking an ordinary Draw action, inspect up to eight random bag souls, choose one, and return the rest. This replaces Favor selection for that draw. The draw ends your turn. You cannot activate this with an empty bag.

Jegudiel — Zeal

After completing your turn, immediately take one additional full turn, provided the round has not ended. Spend the power before starting that extra turn. Then play passes to the next player clockwise. Zeal can follow a Draw action.

Raguel — Judgment

Before your ordinary action, return any positive number K of hand souls to the bag, mix, then blindly draw 2K souls. Returned souls can be redrawn. Choose K only if the bag will contain at least 2K souls after the return. Finish the whole exchange before checking for an empty hand. Then take your ordinary action.

Haniel — The Heavenly Dance

Before your ordinary action, choose a positive integer X no greater than the smallest current hand size. Each player privately selects X hand souls, then all pass them simultaneously to the player on their right (counterclockwise). Selections must be fixed before anyone sees incoming souls. Finish all transfers before checking for an empty hand. Then take your ordinary action. Purgatory and Sanctuary souls cannot be passed. You cannot activate the power if X would be zero.

Gabriel — Calling

Before your ordinary action, name one rank from 1 through 13. All opponents give you every hand soul of that rank. They must comply honestly; their other souls remain private. You need not hold the rank and may call one nobody holds, spending the power. Jokers and souls in Sanctuary or Purgatory are not affected. Scales commitments on a rack are hand souls and must be given if their rank is called. Check for anyone going out after all transfers; if the round continues, take your ordinary action.

Michael — Banishment

Before your ordinary action, choose a rank from 1 through 13 represented in your hand and one opponent. Give them all your hand souls of that rank. Jokers cannot be included. This may empty your hand and end the round immediately; otherwise take your ordinary action.

8. Ordinary Heavenly Decrees

Only one decree is active. Unless specified, Choirs and Processions retain their ordinary requirements, including minimum and maximum sizes.

Decree Complete change
1. Even and Odd Processions advance by exactly 2 each step, in one color, with at least 3 souls. No wrapping. Example: red 2–4–6–8. Ordinary step-1 Processions are not legal.
2. All Are Welcome Choirs still contain exactly 3 or 4 same-rank souls, but colors may repeat.
3. Colorful Processions Processions use consecutive ascending ranks in any colors, with at least 3 souls. No wrapping.
4. Candy Cane Processions use consecutive ascending ranks and alternate exactly two different colors, with at least 3 souls. No wrapping. Example: red 4, blue 5, red 6. Single-color Processions are not legal.
5. The Eternal Circle Same-color Processions may cross 13→1, with at least 3 and at most 13 souls. No rank may repeat within one Procession. Example: blue 12–13–1–2. Nonwrapping Processions remain legal.
6. God's Poker Night Ordinary Choirs and Processions remain legal. Also permit Two Pair (exactly 2 souls of each of two distinct ranks) and Full House (exactly 3 of one rank and 2 of a different rank). Colors may repeat in these added formations. They are neither Choirs nor Processions for powers.
7. The Grand Procession Processions require at least 4 souls, with ordinary color and consecutive-rank rules.
8. Stairway to Heaven Same-color Processions advance by 1 or 2 at each step, independently, with at least 3 souls. Ranks strictly increase; no wrapping. Example: red 3–5–6–8.
9. Heavenly Pairs Choirs may contain 2, 3, or 4 same-rank souls, all different colors.
10. Mirror Choir A Choir is exactly 2 souls of the same color, with different ranks totaling 14: 1+13, 2+12, 3+11, 4+10, 5+9, or 6+8. Ordinary same-rank Choirs and 7+7 are not legal Choirs. Processions are unchanged.

Jokers may fill legal positions in all these formations. Sanctuary powers use the current decree's definition of their named formation type.

9. Final Judgment

Round three uses ordinary formation rules plus one of the following judgments. Determine cumulative standings after round two, before applying the judgment. Every player tied for the highest cumulative score is God's Favorite for this round. This round-three designation is based only on the first two rounds; the game winner is decided after round three.

1. Divine Retribution

If a player who is not a Favorite wins the round, double every Favorite's undelivered penalty before calculating scores. If any Favorite wins, penalties are normal. The winner receives the increased penalties as part of their award. In a stalemate, first choose the stalemate winner using undoubled penalties, then apply doubling if that winner is not a Favorite; do not choose the winner again.

2. Shared Sins

After seeing their starting hand but before the first turn, each player secretly writes down one other player. Reveal all selections at round end. Each receives an independent credit equal to their selected player's penalty loss for the round, before Shared Sins credits. Selecting the round winner earns zero. Several players may select the same person. Credits do not reduce anyone else's loss or increase the round winner's ordinary award.

3. The Scales of Justice

After the deal and before the first turn, each player may set aside 0–5 numbered souls in a separate area of their own rack. These are their committed souls and remain part of their hand. Their separate position identifies them; no tile markers or records of individual tile identities are needed. Jokers cannot be committed.

As soon as a committed soul is introduced into Heaven in a successfully completed legal action, its owner earns a credit equal to its printed rank. Record that credit on the scoresheet immediately: it is final. The delivered soul then becomes an ordinary table soul. Later rearrangement, retrieval, or transfer never erases the credit and never makes that soul committed again. Ordinary Heaven and Sanctuary both qualify as delivery.

Committed souls still set aside on the owner's rack at round end incur triple their normal penalty, replacing the ordinary single penalty. Personal delivery credits do not increase the winner's ordinary award; tripled penalties do.

Transfers: If a power moves a still-committed soul out of its owner's hand without delivering it into Heaven, its commitment ends. It earns no credit and becomes an ordinary soul in its destination. Thus Calling, Banishment, Heavenly Dance, or Judgment require no continuing ownership or identity tracking. If Communion delivers a committed soul directly from its owner's rack into Heaven, the owner receives the permanent credit, while Chamuel receives ordinary Favor for the delivery. Afterward the soul is ordinary, as with any committed delivery.

4. Divine Favor Head Start

Set each player's initial Favor to the number of players with a strictly higher cumulative score: first place gets 0, an untied second gets 1, and so on. Tied players receive the same head start. Continue earning and spending Favor normally.

5. Redemption

After normal round-three scoring, the round winner replaces their single most negative round-one or round-two score with zero. If the two negative scores are equal, erase round one's. If neither is negative, there is no benefit. Recompute that player's cumulative score; no other player's past score changes.

10. Scoring and winning

When someone goes out:

  1. Each other player totals their undelivered souls: printed ranks for numbered tiles and 30 for each joker, including souls in their Purgatory. Souls in Heaven or Sanctuary carry no penalty. Apply any penalty multipliers.
  2. Each other player records the negative of their penalty. The round winner records a positive score equal to the sum of those penalties. A soul in the winner's Purgatory does not reduce that award: Purgatory does not prevent going out, and ordinary finish penalties apply to the other players.
  3. Add any end-of-round personal judgment credits and apply Redemption if relevant. Scales delivery credits have already been recorded when earned; include them in the round total exactly once, without adding them again. These adjustments can make the round's scores sum to a value other than zero.

For a stalemate, use section 6's penalty-difference scoring in step 2 instead. For example, penalties 5, 12, and 20 give the winner +22 and opponents −7 and −15, before personal credits. Divine Retribution first determines the winner from undoubled penalties, then applies any required multiplier before differences.

With an ordinary finish and penalties 8 and 12, scores are +20, −8, and −12. With Divine Retribution doubling the 12, they are +32, −8, and −24. With Shared Sins instead, a player who selected the player losing 12 gains a separate +12 credit; the winner's ordinary +20 award is unchanged.

After round three and all adjustments, the highest cumulative score wins and becomes God's Favorite. A tie for the highest total is a shared victory.
